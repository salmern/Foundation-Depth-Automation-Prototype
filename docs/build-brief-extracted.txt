Reading foundation depths off site drawings
Build brief for pricing
What the client does
Our client is a ground engineering consultancy. When a housebuilder buys a field and plans an estate on
it, they tell the builder how deep to dig the foundations.
Dig too shallow and the house moves. Dig too deep and the builder pours concrete he did not need on
every plot.
What they produce
A spreadsheet. One row per house, four depths per house, one at each corner. A 169 house estate is
676 depths and larger sites run past a thousand houses.
An engineer reads those off the drawings by hand today: 4,056 separate readings on a 169 house site,
and three to four days per site on the drainage alone. We are automating the reading.
What the drawings look like
Nothing is a scan. Every line and every piece of text is a real object on a named layer. They arrive in two
forms and the difference matters for how you price this.
• The client's own foundation drawing is a vector PDF with the layers preserved, so it reads straight
off.
• The developer's engineer's external works and drainage set is DWG, with the topographical survey
inside it as a nested model.
For the test, the free ODA File Converter turns DWG into DXF and open source libraries read DXF
without a licence. For production that converter is licensed for non-commercial use only, so you would
either take an ODA membership or ask the engineer for DXF exports. Tell us which route you would take
and what it costs.
• 1 Plot number, here 453. Every row of the schedule is one of these.
• 2 Finished floor level, printed on the house as FFL= 38.550. The level of the floor slab once the house
is built.
• 3 Drain invert levels, written IL:37.650. The invert is the inside bottom of the pipe. Blue runs are
surface water, red are foul.
• 4 Pipe diameter, written 150 with a diameter symbol. Where it is missing, assume 100.
Ground levels are not on this sheet. They are surveyed spot heights on the topographical survey, which is a
separate drawing.
The four numbers that decide a depth
At each corner of each house, four things compete, and the deepest one wins:
• A minimum depth. In clay this is 0.9 m below the ground, so the house sits below the zone where
clay swells and shrinks.
• Any drain running near the corner. A trench beside a foundation weakens the ground, so the
foundation has to go below the influence of it.
• Any manhole near the corner, for the same reason.
• Any tree near the corner, because roots dry the clay out.
Whichever of those is deepest sets the depth, and the schedule has to say which one it was.

The client issued 97.950 for that corner. The minimum depth rule alone would have given 98.080, so the
drain took it 130 mm deeper. Finding those 130 mm is the entire job.
On that site 245 of 256 rows came back at the same standard depth. The work is finding the 11 that did
not, which means checking every corner to find the few that matter.
What we need built
• Read the drawings and pull out, per house: plot number, floor level, ground level at each corner, and
the nearest drain and manhole to each corner with its invert and distance.
• Apply the rule above and write the answer, plus the reason, into the client's own Excel template.
• Flag the values it could not read cleanly, and say why. An engineer has to be able to check the
handful that matter without re-reading the site.
The same drawings must always give the same schedule. This is a calculation, not an estimate.
What we need back from you

• The test pack sent with this brief, completed.

The spreadsheet you are filling in
The client has their own template and the output goes into it unchanged. Their sheet is called "Generic 4
corner output": one row per house, 58 columns, of two kinds.
• 26 columns hold one value for the whole house: plot number, house type, finished floor level,
average ground level, foundation type, floor slab type, whether a gas or radon membrane is needed.
• 8 columns repeat four times, once per corner, so they occupy 32 columns between them. The two
that matter are Anticipated Bearing Level of Concrete Foundation, which is the depth, and Reason
for Foundation Depth, which says which rule won.
The reason has to be one of seven words, spelled their way: Min Depth, Old Trees, New Tree, Geology,
WPL, Pipe, Manhole. Where a value does not apply their sheet writes 200 rather than leaving it blank.
The drawings that come with it
Four drawings arrive from four firms. Each carries a different part of the answer and none carries all of
it.
• Topographical survey. The ground levels, as bare numbers like 98.76 at the point they were
surveyed. The only drawing that has them.
• Drainage and external works. Finished floor levels, drain and manhole invert levels, pipe diameters.
• Tree survey. Tree positions and the influence rings around them.
• The client's own foundation drawing. Plot numbers, house types, handing, and the corner markers
numbered 1 to 4.
